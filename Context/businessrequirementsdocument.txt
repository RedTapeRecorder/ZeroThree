# **1. Business Overview**
 
Primary purpose — This system is first and foremost a field tool for riders. For new riders, it is a familiarization aid — replacing verbal directions and tribal knowledge with a reliable, visual map of every active outlet in the service area, so that a rider who has never worked this route before can operate independently from day one. For experienced riders, it is an optimization tool — surfacing which outlets have not been visited recently, showing the fastest path through their area, and removing the mental overhead of remembering 300 locations so they can focus on volume and speed. The single measure of whether this system succeeds is whether riders — new and experienced alike — perform better with it than without it.
 
This project is for a business that operates an LPG distribution branch located in San Juan City, Metro Manila, delivering liquefied petroleum gas canisters to sari-sari stores and small retail outlets across San Juan City and its bordering areas — including parts of Quezon City, Manila (Tondo, Sta. Mesa, Pandacan, Sampaloc), and Mandaluyong.
 
The branch has a total known network of approximately 550 recorded outlets, of which a significant portion are inactive, pulled out, or have uncertain status. Of the recorded outlets, approximately 50 to 100 are confirmed active and currently being serviced by 1 to 3 riders traveling daily from the San Juan branch — delivering full canisters and collecting empty ones for refilling. The system is designed to accommodate the full known network as data quality improves over time.
 
The branch conducts two types of transactions with outlets. Refills account for approximately 70% of transactions — the outlet hands over empty canisters and the rider replaces them with full ones at ₱32 per unit, with the empties returned to the branch. New can sales account for approximately 30% — the rider sells a brand new canister to the outlet at ₱70 per unit. Both transaction types may occur within the same visit.
 
# **2. The Problem**
 
## **2.1 Institutional knowledge cannot be transferred to new riders**
 
The branch has built relationships with outlets over years of operation. That knowledge — where each outlet is, what it looks like, how often it orders, who the owner is — currently exists only in the heads of experienced riders and branch staff. When a new rider joins, they receive none of it. There is no map, no outlet profile, no visit history they can consult. They learn by following other riders, by getting lost, and by making mistakes over weeks or months. Given the high turnover rate, this cycle repeats constantly and the business pays for it in lost productivity every time a new rider starts. The primary purpose of this system is to break that cycle — to make institutional knowledge transferable, persistent, and immediately available to any rider from their first day.
 
## **2.2 Knowledge is lost every time a rider leaves**
 
The business has a high rider turnover rate. When an experienced rider leaves, the operational knowledge they carry — which outlets are active, which have relocated, which are difficult to find, which have payment issues — leaves with them entirely. Not everything they know is written down and if it is written down, the writing usually gets forgotten. Each new rider effectively starts nearly from zero.
 
## **2.3 Outlet data quality is poor and inconsistently recorded**
 
The existing outlet records are incomplete and of variable quality. Approximately one fifth of recorded outlets have inaccurate or missing coordinates. A significant portion of recorded outlets have had their LPG product pulled out and are no longer serviceable. Another portion have uncertain status — they may or may not still be active. There is currently no consistent system for recording how confident the branch is in any given outlet record, making it impossible to know which data can be trusted and which needs verification.
 
## **2.4 Managers have no real-time visibility**
 
The branch manager currently has no way to know which outlets have been visited on any given day, which riders are ahead or behind schedule, or which outlets have silently dropped off the active list. All reporting is verbal and after the fact, making it impossible to act on problems while they are still happening.
 
## **2.5 No permanent delivery records per outlet**
 
There is no system linking a specific visit or delivery to a specific outlet on a specific date. This makes it impossible to track which outlets are growing or declining, identify accounts not visited in weeks, or resolve disputes about whether a delivery occurred.
 
## **2.6 No visibility into how riders spend their time**
 
Without any time or activity tracking, the manager has no way to know whether a rider is moving efficiently between outlets, spending excessive time at certain locations, consistently skipping specific outlets, or simply not completing their route. This makes coaching, planning, and dispute resolution impossible.

# **6. Rider Account Model**
 
Rider accounts are created and managed exclusively by the branch manager through the web admin. Riders cannot create, modify, or delete their own accounts.
 
## **6.1 Rider account fields**
 
| **Field** | **Description** | **Editable by** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full name | Rider's legal name | Manager |
| Phone number | Primary contact number | Manager |
| Photo or ID | Profile photo or ID document image | Manager |
| Emergency contact | Name and number of emergency contact | Manager |
| Assigned areas | Barangays or zones the rider covers | Manager |
| Date hired | Date the rider joined the branch | Manager |
| Account status | Active or locked | Manager |
| FCM token | Firebase device token for push notifications — updated automatically by the app | System |
| Days active | Count of days the rider has logged at least one visit — calculated automatically | System |
| Total visits lifetime | Running count of all visits logged — calculated automatically | System |
| Date created | When the account was created | System |
| Last active | Timestamp of the rider's last sync with the server | System |
 
## **6.2 Account creation flow**
 
The manager creates a rider account from the web admin entering all required fields. The system generates a one-time setup code. The rider enters this code on their phone on first launch which triggers the single login event. After that the authentication token is stored on the phone permanently and the rider never logs in again under normal circumstances.
 
## **6.3 Account locking**
 
When the manager locks a rider account from the web admin, the server immediately invalidates the rider's authentication token. The lock takes effect on the rider's phone the moment it next contacts the server. If the phone is online, this is effectively immediate — the app detects the invalid token within seconds and displays a locked screen. If the phone is offline at the moment of locking, the lock takes effect the moment connectivity is restored. This behavior must be clearly communicated to managers — a locked account is not guaranteed to be inaccessible in the field if the rider has no signal. Upon lock, all shift data cached on the phone is wiped.
 
# **7. Outlet Status Model**
 
| **Status** | **Meaning** | **Visible to riders** | **Visible to manager** | **Who can change it** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| active | Currently being serviced by riders | Yes — shown as a pin on the map | Yes | Manager only |
| inactive | Known outlet, not currently being serviced | No — completely hidden | Yes | Manager only |
| pullout | LPG product has been removed from this outlet — no longer serviceable | No — completely hidden | Yes | Manager only |
 
Riders have no ability to change outlet status. If a rider encounters a problem at an outlet — it has moved, shut down, or is refusing deliveries — they report it verbally to the manager, who then updates the status in the web admin. The term pullout is used operationally to describe outlets where the LPG product has been physically removed, distinguishing them from outlets that are merely temporarily inactive.
 
# **7a. Outlet Data Quality Model**
 
Because the branch's existing outlet records are of variable quality, two additional fields are recorded per outlet to help the manager understand how much confidence to place in any given record. These fields are set during import and updated as field verification occurs.
 
## **7a.1 Location pin quality**
 
Records the reliability of the stored GPS coordinates for this outlet.
 
| **Value** | **Meaning** |
| --- | --- |
| precise | Coordinate is exactly at the outlet or the nearest identifiable storefront |
| area | Coordinate places the outlet in the general area but the exact position among nearby stores is uncertain |
| cluster | Multiple outlets are known to be nearby but this specific outlet cannot be individually placed on the map |
| mismatch | The recorded address and the map pin are too far apart to be reliable — coordinate cannot be trusted and requires correction |
| missing | No address and no map pin are available for this outlet |
 
## **7a.2 Location verification level**
 
Records who can confirm the accuracy of this outlet's location and current status.
 
| **Value** | **Meaning** |
| --- | --- |
| auditor | A designated auditor rider has physically confirmed the outlet location and current status |
| rider | A current or former delivery rider can confirm the outlet location and current status |
| staff | Branch staff or the system administrator can confirm the outlet location and current status |
| unverified | No one can currently confirm the outlet location or status — treat with caution |
 
## **7a.3 Combined confidence guide**
 
The two fields work together to indicate how much operational confidence to place in any outlet record. The manager uses this to decide whether an outlet is ready to be shown to riders or needs further verification first.
 
| **Pin quality** | **Verification level** | **Operational meaning** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| precise | auditor | Highest confidence — safe to mark active and show to riders |
| precise | rider or staff | High confidence — safe to mark active |
| area | rider or staff | Moderate confidence — show to riders but flag for future precision improvement |
| cluster | any | Low confidence — keep inactive until a precise coordinate can be obtained |
| mismatch | any | Contradictory data — do not show to riders until coordinate is corrected |
| missing | any | No usable location data — keep inactive |
 
# **8. Outlet Detail Screen**
 
When a rider taps an outlet pin on the map, a detail screen is shown before check-in, containing the following:
 
| **Element** | **Always shown** | **Conditionally shown** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outlet photo | Yes — placeholder if none exists | — |
| Outlet name and address | Yes | — |
| Last visit date | Yes | — |
| Last visit outcome | Yes | — |
| Name of last rider who visited | — | Only if manager has enabled show_last_visitor for this outlet |
| Exact time of last visit | — | Only if manager has enabled show_last_visit_time for this outlet |
 
The two visibility flags are configured independently per outlet by the manager. They default to hidden. Only the single most recent visit is shown — full history is available only in the web admin.
 
# **9. Outlet Photo Management**
 
Each outlet has one current approved photo visible to riders on the detail screen. Photos are stored externally in Cloudinary and referenced by URL in the database — they are never stored as binary data in PostgreSQL.
 
**Manager upload. **The manager may upload a photo for any outlet from the web admin. Manager-uploaded photos are approved immediately with no review step.
 
**Rider submission. **After completing any visit, a rider may optionally submit a new photo. The submission is held in a pending state on the server. The existing approved photo continues showing to all riders. The rider receives no in-app feedback — the submission is silent from their perspective.
 
**Manager review. **A badge counter on the web admin dashboard shows the number of pending photo submissions. The manager opens the queue, reviews the submitted photo alongside the current photo, and approves or rejects. All submissions are retained for audit regardless of outcome.
 
**Photo storage and compression. **Photos are stored in Cloudinary (free tier for v1) and automatically compressed to approximately 200KB on upload. Photos are never stored permanently on rider devices.
 
**Lazy loading. **Photos are fetched only when a rider opens a specific outlet detail screen. They are never included in the shift-start sync payload.
 
# **10. Visit Outcome Model**
 
| **Outcome** | **What happened** | **Delivery logged** | **Time recorded** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| delivered | Rider arrived, conducted one or both transaction types — refills at ₱32 per unit and/or new can sales at ₱70 per unit — and collected payment | Yes | Full arrival and departure |
| no_stock | Rider arrived, owner has no empty canisters to refill | No | Full arrival and departure |
| no_stop | Rider passed through the geofence for under 60 seconds — logged automatically with no rider input | No | Full arrival and departure |
 
Riders may add an optional short text note to any visit outcome. A single delivered visit may involve refill transactions, new can sales, or both. These are tracked as separate unit counts — canisters refilled and new canisters sold — with a single combined payment figure. At least one unit type must be greater than zero for a delivered outcome to be valid. The no_stop outcome is detected automatically when a rider passes through the geofence radius for fewer than 60 seconds.
 
# **11. Geofence and Time Tracking**
 
## **11.1 Geofence radius**
 
The system uses a configurable 20-meter geofence radius per outlet for check-in verification, chosen to account for typical consumer Android GPS drift of 15 to 25 meters in dense urban environments. If the rider is outside this radius at check-in, a warning is displayed but they may still proceed. GPS accuracy at check-in is stored with every visit record for audit purposes.
 
The geofence radius is stored as a configurable system parameter in the database defaulting to 20 meters, subject to review and adjustment during field testing in Metro Manila without requiring any app redeployment. When the rider enters the geofence radius, a 60-second timer starts. If the rider remains inside for more than 60 seconds, the app prompts them to log a visit outcome. If the rider exits in under 60 seconds, a no_stop outcome is recorded automatically.
 
To preserve battery life, GPS position is polled at 10-second intervals rather than streamed continuously. The poll interval is a configurable system parameter. GPS polling consumes no mobile data — the GPS chip receives satellite signals passively without requiring an internet connection. A-GPS almanac data of approximately 10KB is downloaded once per day during the shift-start WiFi sync.
 
## **11.2 Time recording**
 
- Arrival time recorded when rider enters the geofence radius
 
- Departure time recorded when rider exits the geofence radius
 
- Duration in minutes calculated automatically from arrival and departure timestamps
 
- All times recorded in Philippine Standard Time (PST, UTC+8)
 
# **12. Route Model**
 
The manager may create a suggested route and assign it to a specific rider at any time through the web admin. Routes are ad hoc — there is no fixed schedule or automatic generation. A route contains up to 80 active outlets presented to the rider in a suggested sequence. The sequence is a recommendation only — riders may deviate at their discretion based on conditions on the ground.
 
**Note: ***A rider carries approximately 4 × 33 canisters per load. With each outlet taking 5 to 10 or more canisters, a rider exhausts their supply after a small number of stops and must return to the branch to reload. The actual daily visit sequence is dictated by load cycles and reload availability, not a fixed plan. Route deviation is a physical necessity, not just a preference.*
 
Within any route, the manager may flag individual outlets as high priority. High priority outlets are communicated to the rider via push notification through Firebase Cloud Messaging and a distinct visual marker on the outlet's map pin. Routes are included in the shift-start sync payload and subject to the same encrypted temporary storage and automatic wipe at shift end.
 
# **13. Data Privacy Considerations**
 
Visit activity and rider location data fall under the Philippine Data Privacy Act (Republic Act 10173).
 
**Informed consent at onboarding. **A disclosure screen on first login states that visit activity — including arrival time, departure time, duration, and visit outcome — is recorded and visible to the branch manager. The rider must acknowledge this before proceeding.
 
**Defined purpose. **Data is collected solely for route planning, delivery verification, and operational efficiency review.
 
**Data minimization and device security. **The system uses a 20-meter geofence and samples GPS only at geofence entry and exit — not continuously. Outlet data is encrypted on the device during the shift using a session key issued by the server. The session key is revoked at logout or account lock, rendering any remaining cached data unreadable. All outlet data is wiped from the device at shift end or logout.
 
**Access control. **Visit data is visible only to the branch manager and system administrator. It is not shared externally.
 
**Legal note: ***A qualified Philippine lawyer should review the time-tracking and device data features before deployment to real employees to confirm compliance with RA 10173. The time-tracking feature is designed to be disableable without requiring a rebuild or redeployment of the core application.*

# **18. Success Definition**
 
**For new riders: **A rider who has never worked this route before opens the app on their first day, completes the one-time setup using the manager-provided code, acknowledges the data collection disclosure, sees only active outlets with reliable coordinates as pins on a map, taps an outlet to see its photo and last visit information, navigates there using their existing map app, checks in within the geofence radius, logs a delivery, and that visit appears in the manager's web dashboard — all without asking anyone for directions.
 
**For experienced riders: **A rider who already knows the area opens the app at shift start, immediately sees which nearby outlets have gone the longest without a visit, follows or deviates from their assigned route at their discretion, and completes more outlet visits per day than they did before the app existed.
 
**For the manager: **The branch manager opens the web admin, creates a new rider account in under two minutes, assigns a route in under five minutes, can see all visit activity across all riders in real time, can lock any rider account with effect at the next reconnect, can identify any active outlet not visited in over 7 days, and can filter all outlets by data quality level to build a verification work queue — all from a single browser window.
 
**For the system: **Daily operation uses under 5MB of mobile data per rider. Outlet data never permanently resides on any rider device. The app functions fully offline for visit logging and enforces all security constraints the moment connectivity is restored.
